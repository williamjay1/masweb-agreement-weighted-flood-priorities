# Missouri NOAA VIIRS geolocation follow-up, 15 November 2026

## Conclusion and scope

The May 30 NOAA VIIRS 1-day NetCDF and GeoTIFF contain the same 4,448 x 4,448 categorical array; their source georeferencing differs materially. The NetCDF latitude and longitude arrays, read as pixel centres by GDAL, lead to different pixel assignments from the supplied GeoTIFF. Product-specific metadata alone does not establish which convention correctly describes the producer's actual processing. A new retrospective comparison against historic JRC Landsat surface-water occurrence provides a useful check but **does not resolve the discrepancy and is not flood validation**.

## Original product and coordinate conventions

Inputs are immutable source files at `external/data/Geocarto_R2_20261115/raw/missouri_noaa_viirs_20261115/`. The NetCDF global attribute says `Conventions=CF-1.6, ACDD-1.3`, has lat/lon dimensions with standard_name and units but no bounds attribute or boundary variable, and specifies geographic global extrema W=-105, E=-90, S=30, N=45 degrees. The first NetCDF longitude/latitude coordinates are -105 and 45 degrees and their final values are approximately -90.004715 and 30.004715. The stored nominal step is approximately 0.003372 degrees. The GeoTIFF is `AREA_OR_POINT=Area`, its outer bounds are (-105,30,-90,45), and its geotransform puts (-105,45) at the upper-left outer boundary; its grid spacing is about 0.003372302 degrees. The NetCDF subdataset transform inferred by GDAL uses the coordinate arrays as grid locations, not the outer edge.

The mismatch therefore combines a different origin interpretation and slightly different spacing: it is not globally a constant half pixel. At source row 2211, column 3352, the NetCDF interpretation gives (-93.69705522, 37.54450749), whereas the GeoTIFF interpretation gives (-93.69435701, 37.54215378). The WGS84 ellipsoidal inverse distance from `pyproj.Geod(ellps="WGS84").inv` is 353.70 m. Other flood-class cells 2137,3447 and 2212,3352 have respective distances 353.05 and 353.73 m. These distances compare coordinate interpretations, not measured sensor geolocation errors.

CF-1.6 Section 7.1 describes cell bounds separately from coordinates (https://cfconventions.org/Data/cf-conventions/cf-conventions-1.6/build/cf-conventions.html); GDAL explains how it derives a transform from coordinate variables (https://gdal.org/en/stable/drivers/raster/netcdf.html). Neither source establishes the intentions of this specific operational file producer. Producer confirmation and/or genuinely independent control points are needed.

## Retrospective geographic diagnostic using historical water

The reference is JRC/Google Global Surface Water v1.5 water occurrence 1984–2024, tile 100W_40N, downloaded 15 November 2026 from https://storage.googleapis.com/water-world/download2024/VER1-5/occurrence/occurrence_100W_40N_v1_5_2024.tif . The original file remains at `external/data/Geocarto_R2_20261115/raw/missouri_viirs_geolocation_20261115/occurrence_100W_40N_v1_5_2024.tif`, size 67,345,756 bytes, SHA256 `438245ce4c66b248c1453edf7505bc6173a388602deb326803e82341af927d52`. The official guide https://storage.googleapis.com/water-world/downloads_ancillary/DataUsersGuidev2024_v.5.pdf defines 0 as no observed water, values 1–100 as water occurrence percentage, and 255 as no data. We coded >=95 as frequently observed historical water, masked 255, and treated remaining values as not meeting this particular threshold.

We used the same NOAA category-array entries under both grid definitions within the original event bounding box, inset two NOAA grid cells to avoid comparing different edge samples. Classes were fixed *before* this geographic overlap comparison at NOAA 99 (normal water; 2,925 cells) and NOAA 17 (vegetation; 183,649 cells). For each NOAA grid definition the 0/1 JRC mask was area-averaged to each NOAA cell with rasterio/GDAL `Resampling.average`; the reported statistic is the equal-cell mean of the resulting high-occurrence fraction, not an event flood accuracy, ecological area-weighted estimate or independent-sample significance test. For 99 normal water the means are 0.457465 with the NetCDF grid and 0.472857 with the supplied GeoTIFF grid. For 17 vegetation, corresponding means are 0.000660 and 0.000311. Adjacent cells are spatially dependent; daily water state, historic Landsat observation, mixed pixels, reference-water masks, positional errors, and threshold all influence these values. These are event-box-wide cells, **not** the 5,661 selected optical centres.

The NOAA VFM user manual v3.2 table 2-3 (https://cimss.ssec.wisc.edu/csppgeo/includes/documents/Enterprise_Flood_Mapping_External_Users_Manual.pdf) lists a global reference water mask based on MOD44W, an ESA global water mask, and NLCD 2006. The document does not identify the exact ESA mask enough to rule JRC data in or out. NLCD and JRC both involve Landsat imagery, and neither product is independent field survey truth. Therefore this is contextual geometric triangulation, not a blind independent validation of the source data.

An exploratory nine-point shift sweep of the GeoTIFF transform used +/-0.5 source cell in each direction. The greatest normal-water overlap among those nine was 0.491905 at -0.5 columns, 0 rows, exceeding the published GeoTIFF value 0.472857. This result was selected post hoc, does not estimate geolocation truth, is not intended as a proposed correction, and illustrates why maximizing historic-water overlap cannot settle the source coordinate convention. Full results are in `alignment_grid_search.json` and reproducible code in `grid_search.py`. The two originally published-grid comparisons are in `permanent_water_alignment.json`; code is `compare_permanent_water_alignment.py`. Both use the archived source imagery without changing it. Computed `.npy` grids are intermediate output on D.

## Practical interpretation

Continue reporting the originally recorded 742/3 NetCDF and 743/5 GeoTIFF sampled-grid results as two alternative published definitions, noting only 1 common flood-positive 30 m centre, rather than selecting either grid as verified or deriving an accuracy estimate. The historical water diagnostic is optional supplementary context; its limited and dependent nature means it cannot elevate the Missouri independent flood-reference claim.
