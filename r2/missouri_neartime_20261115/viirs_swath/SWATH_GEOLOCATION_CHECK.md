# Same-day NOAA-20 swath geolocation follow-up

## Finding and limits

A pair of 375 m NOAA-20 VIIRS granules from the same orbit and acquisition time covers the Missouri event rectangle on 30 May 2025. Its original terrain-corrected latitudes and longitudes and I2 observations provide a same-day geometrical cross-check on the two published grids. This exploratory diagnostic does **not** identify which source georeferencing is producer-intended. It is not an independent flood validation and does not establish the accuracy of either product.

The NetCDF states `time_coverage_start=2025-05-30T17:37:14Z` and `time_coverage_end=2025-05-30T21:00:37Z`; its filename encodes the same start and end. The NOAA-20 paired files encode start `20:05:10.4` UTC, end `20:06:34.9` UTC, orbit `39023`. Thus the observation time falls within the composite metadata window. This does not prove that this particular pass contributed to any particular composite cell. The NetCDF lists `platform=NPP,N20,N21` but has no per-pixel observation-time or satellite identifier variable. Daily compositing can select data from multiple overpasses; a reported date window is not a pixel-level provenance record.

The nearest geolocation sample in this swath to (-93.6970, 37.5445) is at (-93.6953, 37.546238), approximately 244 m away using the stated local approximation. The swath intersects the original event rectangle, and only its samples within the rectangle are used below. The swath and composite are not independent instruments: NOAA identifies I2 and GITCO among VIIRS flood algorithm inputs. The I2 sample and the final daily product may also differ in time or contributing sensor, despite compatibility with the full time window.

## Comparison

We sampled the same NOAA-20 swath pixel centres from the original event box [-94.1241, 36.4828, -92.5184, 37.8490]. We retained `QF1_VIIRSIBANDSDR == 0` and integer I2 reflectance below 65528, and used the source reflectance scale and offset: `I2 reflectance = raw × 2.4417415e-05 + 0`. These 85,601 observations are swath pixel centres within the study rectangle and swath coverage, not the original 5,661 optical sample centres. QF1 comprises SDR calibration, saturation, missing-data and out-of-range flags, so zero is a strict radiometric quality filter; **it is not a cloud mask**. We did not obtain a matching same-pass cloud mask, and did not construct one from a single band. The composite vegetation and normal-water codes describe composite pixels, not necessarily the sky condition of this swath pass. Each swath pixel centre was assigned separately to the original daily category array under the NetCDF-coordinate grid (GDAL transform) and the published GeoTIFF grid; neither grid was shifted or tuned in this comparison.

Of 85,601 swath samples meeting these conditions, 2,288 receive different categorical values under the two coordinate interpretations. For the full assigned 99 normal-water class, the 1,314 NetCDF and 1,312 GeoTIFF assignments have mean I2 reflectance 0.23264 and 0.23491 respectively. The 854 samples classified as 99 under both definitions have mean 0.20152. For the two paired disagreement subsets, NetCDF 99 versus GeoTIFF 17 vegetation consists of 211 samples with mean 0.31887, and GeoTIFF 99 versus NetCDF 17 consists of 297 samples with mean 0.32331. Their reflectance distributions overlap greatly (10th–90th percentiles respectively 0.18645–0.45973 and 0.17263–0.50664). These groups are not the same samples, differ in size, and the samples are spatially dependent. This is an exploratory descriptive comparison selected after the positional discrepancy was found, not a preregistered test or an accuracy estimate. Mixed pixels, clouds or shadows, varying view geometry, compositing from other overpasses, and land/water spectral overlap all limit interpretation. Differences do not resolve the correct coordinate interpretation, and no best-grid claim is warranted.

## Official producer evidence and access

NOAA's [VIIRS Flood Mapping Algorithm Theoretical Basis Document](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/jpss/documents/ATBD/ATBD_VIIRS_Flood_Mapping_v1.0.pdf), section 2.1, describes projection using VIIRS I-bands and terrain-corrected GITCO, gridded flood detection and daily mosaicking, then an image-display step producing GeoTIFF and shapefile from the NetCDF. It supports the relationship between these products but does not document this file's exact pixel centre/corner and step choices. The [2025 Enterprise Flood Mapping external user's manual](https://cimss.ssec.wisc.edu/csppgeo/includes/documents/Enterprise_Flood_Mapping_External_Users_Manual.pdf) confirms the filenames, input data, and classes, but does not resolve the coordinate discrepancy. The NOAA [VIIRS SDR product format](https://ncc.nesdis.noaa.gov/documents/documentation/viirs-sdr-dataformat.pdf), VIIRS I-band SDR quality flag table, defines the components of QF1.

The University of Wisconsin [CSPP VIIRS flood software description](https://cimss.ssec.wisc.edu/cspp/viirs_flood_v2.0.shtml) states that its Version 2.0 package includes scripts for NetCDF-to-GeoTIFF output; the described package is adapted for direct broadcast single-granule use, so even its implementation is not necessarily proof of the historical NOAA operational daily-composite code. The [CSPP download page](https://cimss.ssec.wisc.edu/cspp/download/) requires registration or an email login; no registration or login was attempted. Direct producer clarification, source-implementation inspection, or a well-controlled check with exact per-pixel provenance remains needed to settle the convention.
