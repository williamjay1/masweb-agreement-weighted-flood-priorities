# SWOT PIXC observation comparison plan (pre-analysis)

Scope: compare independent KaRIn surface-water detections on 2025-05-28 with the pre-existing S30/L30 optical classifications on 2025-05-30 over their fixed jointly valid agricultural pixel-centre sample. Both acquisition dates postdate the pre-registered May 23–27 event window. The comparison cannot directly establish event-peak flood accuracy or a reference confusion matrix for the entire study area.

Inputs: eight exact Version D granules listed in `SWOT_PIXC_granule_manifest.csv`, after exact filenames, byte counts, and NASA MD5 hashes are verified; `external/analysis/Geocarto_R2_20261115/independent_validation_20261115/missouri_followup/rtc_may26_point_samples.csv`, which supplies original EPSG:5070 centres and optical classifications; and `external/analysis/Geocarto_R2_20261115/missouri_swot_new_footprints_20261115/point_nominal_footprint_membership.csv`. Record provenance, per-file status, acquisition, versions, and comparison exclusions.

1. Inspect NetCDF groups, dimensions, field attributes, fill values, geolocation and variable-specific quality-bit meanings from each actual granule and official PDD. Read in bounded chunks. Keep passes separate and ensure adjacent granule points are not counted as independent optical sample cells. Project valid longitude/latitude to EPSG:5070.
2. Tabulate classes and exact quality flags per pass. Main water sensitivity uses class 3 (water near land) and 4 (open water) with geolocation_qual and classification_qual equal to zero; report counts before and after every filter. A separate analysis may include class 5 (dark water) and other classes only with explicit scientific justification, supported by bitwise flag interpretation and transparent quality criteria. Never conflate bitwise flags with four-level summary variables.
3. Preserve the original fixed optical sample and nominal pass masks. Attribute PIXC points to the 30 m squares centred on the original optical centres; report how many unique sample squares contain quality-screened detected water points, and the S30/L30 classification distributions among these matched squares. Pass-specific counts first, pooled unique sample squares only after documenting deduplication. A point location in a cell is not full areal overlap; pixel geolocation errors and differing sensor footprint and spatial resolution must be acknowledged. If warranted, separately report prespecified proximity sensitivities, without tuning thresholds to improve agreement.
4. The data are a selectively retained point cloud, not wall-to-wall land/water observations. No PIXC water detection at a pixel centre must remain unobserved or indeterminate, not a dry negative. Do not report full-domain specificity, overall accuracy, IoU, commission/omission, or complete independent flood validation without a separately justified valid-observation domain and observed negative reference. Even class 1 or 2 appears only in a selected subset and cannot be generalized to the whole domain. Nominal swath polygons do not establish pixel coverage.
5. If an independently documented prior-water dataset is already in the project, compare detected locations to permanent-water areas as a separate descriptive stratification, recording source, date, scale, thresholds, and positional uncertainty. One water detection alone does not establish flood inundation. SWOT classification also incorporates prior information, so sensor independence does not imply prior-data independence.
6. Report S30 and L30 separately, denominators including no valid PIXC data and screened-out cases, exact time differences, spatial limitations and low positive counts; interpret agreement as an exploratory observation conditional on selected detections, not proof of flood map accuracy. Document differences and limits in manuscript and responses only after verified analysis.
