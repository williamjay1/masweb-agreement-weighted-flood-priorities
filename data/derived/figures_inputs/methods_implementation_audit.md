# Methods implementation audit for the Geocarto first revision

Audit date: 2026-10-07. Route: SCI applied remote sensing / decision screening. Scope: read-only source and derived-data inspection, with a new timestamp table. No raw source was changed and no raster processing was rerun. The submitted manuscript source is `geocarto_revision_v2_20260820/editor_revision_20260825_v1/MASWEB_Geocarto_Main_Anonymous.tex`, not the older current `revision_geocarto_v4/manuscript` text. Paths below are relative to the project `candidate3_agricultural_hazard_cascade` unless stated otherwise.

## Verified implementation and corrections

1. **MASWEB full name.** `revision_geocarto_v4/release/MASWEB_v2_anonymous/README.md:3` identifies the **Multiscale Agricultural Surface Water Equivalence Benchmark**. This is an established name, not an inferred expansion.

2. **Agricultural pixel eligibility.** `revision_geocarto_v4/scripts/build_blind_event_eligibility.py:107` takes the three years before the event, excluding the event year. Lines 394–410 require CDL class 1 or 5 (corn or soybean) in at least two of those years, valid JRC occurrence and seasonality data, neither occurrence >=75% nor seasonality >=10 months, and membership in the frozen event geometry. The domain was constructed from affected counties intersected with both families' tile footprints. This rule does not identify all agricultural classes or require a crop standing on the acquisition date. It should be described as a recent stable corn/soybean domain.

3. **Grid and resampling stages.** Eligibility uses EPSG:5070 at 30 m (`build_blind_event_eligibility.py:49,259`). JRC layers are aligned by nearest neighbour (`:352`). DSWx source rasters are aligned to the eligibility grid using `WarpedVRT(..., resampling=Resampling.nearest)` in `build_expanded_pair_states.py:128–139`; the blind-state wrapper explicitly invokes this implementation (`build_blind_pair_states.py:66–82`). Thus the original blanket sentence “No water state was ... resampled” is false unless restricted to the later 500 m and coarser aggregation step. HLS reflectance bands in the algorithm comparison are likewise aligned by nearest neighbour (`build_hls_index_common_support.py:209–223`). The paper uses 30 m HLS/DSWx products; 500 m is an analytical reporting cell, not native satellite resolution. An official HLS product citation is needed if the revised text also explains the upstream resampling of Sentinel-2 native bands.

4. **30 m pixels to 500 m cells.** `build_common_support_cells.py:91–101` assigns pixel centres to `floor(x/500),floor(y/500)`. Lines 121–182 sum counts and form water fractions using common-valid pixel counts. No bilinear interpolation or averaging of class codes creates the 500 m grid. Grid edges do not split a 30 m pixel: centre assignment is used. Therefore counts can be 16x16, 16x17 or 17x17 pixels before eligibility masking; do not equate count*900 with an exactly clipped 250,000 m2 cell area. The valid denominator is the pixel count, not the full geometric area.

5. **A 500 m cell need not be wholly cropland.** The strict coverage ratio is common clean eligible pixel count / all eligible pixel count in that cell (`build_common_support_cells.py:149–159`; `build_primary_analysis_database.py:97`). It is not a requirement that 95% of the full 500 m cell be agricultural. There is no minimum cropland area/share requirement in the original pipeline. Direct reading of `primary_500m_cells.parquet`, retaining blind events and coverage >=0.95, gives 44,510 cells; 817 have one eligible pixel and 1,962 have fewer than ten. Counts range 1–289. Explain this boundary transparently; do not invent a minimum area criterion retrospectively. These small-support cells receive their actual common-pixel weights during count aggregation. Their effect on fine-scale ranking can be acknowledged or tested separately if useful.

6. **Strict QA and water states.** `build_expanded_pair_states.py:85` uses DSWx codes 1, 2, 3 for primary water. Lines 207–214 encode cloud, snow, ocean, uncovered, primary/expanded overlap conflict, unknown classes and nonfill status. `build_common_support_cells.py:31–32,109–117` excludes every bad bit and requires nonfill in both chains. Pixels must be eligible and clean/valid in both. The full candidate domain includes dry cells for the current primary scale analysis; older `run_multiscale_scale_analysis.py` is the historical active-cell branch and should not be cited as the current all-cell analysis.

7. **Coarser square units.** `geocarto_revision_v2_20260820/scripts/analyze_registered_scale_sensitivity.py:97` assigns 500 m cell centres by `floor(x/scale_m),floor(y/scale_m)`, then sums numerator and denominator counts. Ten, twenty and thirty km are nominal square side lengths. For retained cells g in unit h, N_h=sum N_g, W^S_h=sum W^S_g, W^L_h=sum W^L_g; S_h=W^S_h/N_h and L_h=W^L_h/N_h. This is equivalent to a common-pixel-weighted mean of cell fractions, not an unweighted mean. Empty/unsupported portions do not enter the denominator. There is no interpolation in this aggregation step.

8. **County AWC uses pooled counts, not a mean of cell AWC.** `revision_geocarto_v4/scripts/run_operational_unit_validation.py:453–460` retains all strict cells, including dry cells, and constructs point geometries from their centres. Lines 154–174 join centres to county polygons (smallest FIPS would resolve a boundary tie); :127–151 sum N, S-water, L-water, intersection and union by county. County scores are computed from these pooled counts. `geocarto_revision_v2_20260820/inner_data_audit_20260820/positive_utility_experiment/run_positive_utility_experiment.py:129–139` recomputes consensus and AWC after loading these county records. For county c:

   N_c=sum_g N_g; S_c=(sum_g W^S_g)/N_c; L_c=(sum_g W^L_g)/N_c; I_c=sum_g I_g; U_c=sum_g U_g; J_c=I_c/U_c if U_c>0, otherwise 0; AWC_c=(S_c+L_c)J_c/2.

   Each cell contributes its complete sufficient statistics to the county containing its centre; no polygon clipping of individual cell contents is performed. Coarse 10/20/30 km rankings are a parallel scale experiment, not an intermediate input to county AWC. The source county table and the V2 audit copy have been compared directly: 830 rows x 15 columns, `pandas.DataFrame.equals=True`. `results/analysis/operational_validation/spatial_linkage_audit.csv` reports 88,490 of 88,490 strict cells assigned, zero unassigned and zero duplicate boundary rows across the nine-event database.

9. **Budget rules must be separated.** The county utility function `run_positive_utility_experiment.py:37–59,147–154` allocates exactly q*n county equivalents, with full weight above the cutoff and the remaining weight divided equally among every county tied at the cutoff. It does not use coordinate order to break county utility ties. Capture=sum_c w_c Y_c / sum_c Y_c; lift=capture/q. By contrast, scale-set overlap uses an integer k=ceil(q*n), with coordinate tie ordering (`analyze_registered_scale_sensitivity.py:83–94`; current primary function should be traced to `analyze_historical_hls_sar_utility.py`). The submitted Methods 2.4 replaced both with an exact-county statement and is internally inconsistent with integer Top-k sets. Explain each separately and define q, n, k and w explicitly. Fractional selection is an evaluation allocation/probability convention, not a claim that a county is physically divided.

10. **Spectral definitions.** `build_hls_index_common_support.py:253–270` maps L30 blue/green/NIR/SWIR1/SWIR2 to B02/B03/B05/B06/B07 and S30 to B02/B03/B8A/B11/B12. MNDWI=(G-SWIR1)/(G+SWIR1), with zero/near-zero denominator excluded; fixed water threshold >0. The implemented AWEI is the shadow variant, AWEI_sh=B+2.5G-1.5(NIR+SWIR1)-0.25SWIR2, threshold >0, not AWEI_nsh. All required bands must be nonfill and Fmask bits 0–4 clear (:53,251). Overlapping tiles with conflicting index states are removed (:273–283). Four-chain valid support means strict DSWx in both sensors plus valid HLS index inputs in both; all product fractions use the same four-chain common count (:310–328,416–428). The algorithm-sensitivity active rank domains can differ because product-specific water union differs, even after the common pixel support is held constant. Do not explain every active-event difference solely as differing cloud masks.

11. **Mechanism definitions in the submitted text are wrong.** Current `revision_geocarto_v4/scripts/analyze_spatial_mechanisms.py:90,102,106–109` computes within-sensor score SD(scale)/SD(500 m) separately for S30 and L30, and Spearman correlation between each 500 m cell score and its containing block score. Rank persistence is a correlation, not a fraction of units with unchanged ordering. The SD metric is not the SD of S30-minus-L30 discrepancy. The reported S30 0.494 and L30 0.697 are compatible with separate score SD ratios. Correct Methods, Table 2, Results, Discussion and the figure caption/axis label accordingly; numerical results need not change when accurately relabelled.

## Ready-to-use Methods wording

**Agricultural domain and base grid.** “Agricultural eligibility was assigned at the 30 m analysis-pixel level. A pixel had to be classified as corn or soybean (CDL classes 1 or 5) in at least two of the three years before the event, fall inside the frozen event domain, and have valid JRC surface-water records without persistent water (occurrence >=75% or seasonality >=10 months). The event-year CDL was not used. Thus, eligibility represents recent stable corn/soybean land and does not imply that a crop was present on the acquisition date. The HLS-derived DSWx observations were aligned to the EPSG:5070 30 m eligibility grid using nearest-neighbour resampling. Pixel centres were then assigned to a globally anchored 500 m square grid. The 500 m cells are analytical units, not sensor pixels.”

**Support and aggregation.** “Let A_g be the number of eligible agricultural pixels in cell g and N_g the number clean and valid in both sensor chains. A cell was retained when N_g>0 and N_g/A_g>=0.95. This threshold concerns observation completeness within eligible agricultural land; it neither requires the full 500 m cell to be cropland nor uses the full cell area as a denominator. Each water fraction divides water-pixel count by N_g. Coarser square units were formed by summing the same counts from retained base cells and recomputing the fractions. No water-state interpolation was applied during aggregation from 500 m to coarser units.”

**County score.** “The county analysis branches directly from the retained 500 m cells. Each cell was assigned by its centre to a county, and its common-valid, sensor-water, joint-water and union-water counts were summed with those of other assigned cells. The county fractions and Jaccard weight were recomputed from these pooled counts before AWC was calculated. County AWC is therefore a nonlinear score of aggregated counts, rather than an average of cell-level AWC. Counties with zero mapped water received AWC=0. The fixed-budget utility calculation assigned a total weight qn across n candidate counties; all counties above the cutoff received weight one, and the remaining weight was shared equally among cutoff ties. Fractional weights preserve the same nominal budget in events with different numbers of counties.”

**Mechanisms.** “For each sensor, rank persistence was the Spearman correlation between the original 500 m scores and the scores of their containing coarser units, mapped back to the same base cells. The within-sensor score SD ratio divided the standard deviation across coarse-unit scores by the corresponding standard deviation at 500 m. These diagnostics quantify preservation of within-sensor ranking and attenuation of within-sensor score variability; they do not estimate a causal mechanism or directly measure the variance of the between-sensor discrepancy.”
